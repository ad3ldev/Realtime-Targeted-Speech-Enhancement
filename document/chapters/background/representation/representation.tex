\section{Audio Representation \cite{manilow2020}}
Starting the examination of input and output representations fundamentals to speech enhancement systems, we turn our attention to the fundamentals of audio data. A waveform is the most basic type of audio, which is generated when a microphone detects air pressure fluctuations and converts them into an electrical signal. While some speech improvement approaches work directly on this waveform, a significant share require preparatory preprocessing processes before engaging in substantive noise reduction procedures. This section carefully examines the many types of input and output representations used in speech enhancement techniques.

Our research begins with a thorough examination of waveforms, which are regarded as the most fundamental representation of audio. Following that, we examine commonly used audio representations that are specifically designed for speech enhancement applications.
