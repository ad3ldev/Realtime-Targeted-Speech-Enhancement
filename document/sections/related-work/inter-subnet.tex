\subsection{Inter-Subnet}
Chen et al. \cite{chen2023intersubnet} took the subband approach to solve the single channel speech enhancement problem,
building upon the work of Hao et al.\cite{fullsubnet}.
The authors of the paper proposed a subband interaction moddule to capture the inter-subband dependencies with lower number of parameters.
InterSubNet is a novel, lightweight framework for speech enhancement. It alternates between a SubInter module and a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) layer.
This design allows it to grasp the overall spectral information while maintaining its capacity to concentrate on local spectral patterns.

\subsubsection{Model Architecture}
Considering the short-time fourier transform STFT of a single-channel signal with 16 kHz sampling rate:
\begin{equation}
     X_{t,f} =  S_{t,f} + N_{t,f}
\end{equation}
where $S_{t,f}$ is the clean speech and $N_{t,f}$ is the noise. The network is trained to estimate the clean speech $S_{t,f}$ from the noisy speech $X_{t,f}$.

The complete structure of Inter-SubNet $G_{is}$ is illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:inter-subnet}. It takes as input a magnitude spectrogram $X \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times T} $.
Here, $F$ and $T$ denote the total count of frequency bins and frames, respectively. The first step transforms the spectrogram into subband units $\{\text{{\textbf{b}}}_i\}_{i=1}^{F}$ using an "unfold" operation.
These subband units are then input into the $G_{is}$ model, which generates the final outputs cIRM $M^r$ and $M^i$. The main structure of $G_is$ includes two stacked SubInter-LSTM (SIL) blocks, followed by a single fully-connected layer.

\paragraph{Subband units}\mbox{}\\
Subband units are derived from the magnitude spectrogram $X$ using the "unfold" operation and are used as the input for the Inter-SubNet.
Specifically, for each frequency $i$ from the magnitude spectrogram $X$, a frequency bin vector $X_i \in \mathbb{R}^T$ is taken,
along with the $2n$ frequency bin vectors adjacent to it, to form a subband unit $b_i$. This can be represented as:

\begin{equation}
    b_i = [X_{i-n}, ..., X_i, ..., X_{i+n}] \in \mathbb{R}^{Fs \times T}
\end{equation}

Here, "[, ]" signifies the concatenation operation and $Fs = 2n + 1$. Additionally, for boundary frequencies where $i + n > F - 1$ or $i - n < 0$,
circular Fourier frequencies are assigned. Through this process, $F$ subband units are obtained, each with a dimension of $(Fs, T)$.
These are denoted as $\{\textbf{b}_i\}_{i=1}^{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times Fs}$.

\paragraph{SubInter module}\mbox{}\\
Inspired by \cite{luo2020endtoend}, The SubInter module is designed to extract global spectral information, which includes dependencies across different bands and overall spectral patterns.
This is achieved through the interaction among the parallel subband units. Additionally, this module integrates the extracted global spectral information back into these subband units.
Having a collection of $F$ subband units $\{b_i\}_{i=1}^{F}$, where each $b_i \in \mathbb{R}^{Fs \times T}$ and $Fs$ denotes the number of frequency bins in a subband unit.
The SubInter module operates on these subband units to incorporate complementary global spectral information:

\begin{equation}
    \{\hat{\textbf{b}}_i\}_{i=1}^{F} = H(\{\textbf{b}_i\}_{i=1}^{F})
\end{equation}

Here, $\hat{\textbf{b}}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{Fs \times T}$ is the subband feature that encapsulates both global spectral information and local spectral patterns,
and $H(\cdot)$ is the mapping function that characterizes the SubInter module.

\subsubsection{Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Dataset}\mbox{}\\
The authors used a subset of the DNS Challenge - Interspeech 2021 dataset for training. Room impulse repsonses were randomly sampled and added to 75\% of the clean speech.
Afterwards, the noisy speeches were mixed with the clean or reverberant speeches at a randomly selected SNR varying from -5 to 20 db.

\paragraph{Training}\mbox{}\\
STFT with a Hanning window with length of 32 ms and a hop size of 16 ms was used to obtain the magnitude spectrogram.
The model was trained for 1000 epochs with the Adam optimizer with a learning rate $1e^{-3}$, a batch size of 40 (2 gpus with 20 samples each) and $MSE$ loss.
The authors used subband units with $n = 15$ (i.e, 15 neighbor frequencies are taken on each side of each input frequency bin).
In the training procedure, the input-target sequence pairs were generated into a constant length sequence of $T = 192$ frames (approximately 3 s).

\subsubsection{Conclusion}
This paper presents Subband Interaction (SubInter) module, a novel approach to modeling global spectral information for speech enhancement, and introduces a new framework called Inter-SubNet. This innovative,
lightweight framework for speech enhancement is engineered to leverage global spectral information while maintaining its capacity to concentrate on local spectral patterns.
